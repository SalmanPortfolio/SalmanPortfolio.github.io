        <h2>🌐 My Digital Footprint as a Computer Engineering Student</h2>

        <p>
            Every journey leaves a mark. Some marks fade with time, while others persist, grow,
            and eventually become something you can look back on with pride.
            As a student of <strong>Computer Engineering</strong>, I have started building
            something that I hope will stand the test of time—a personal portfolio blog hosted on
            <a href="https://pages.github.com/" target="_blank">
                <strong>GitHub Pages</strong>
            </a>.
            This is not just a class assignment or a box to check on a to-do list.
            It is the beginning of my digital identity, a space where my learning,
            my struggles, my breakthroughs, and my growth as an engineer will be recorded
            for the world to see.
        </p>

        <p>
            The idea for this portfolio came from my professor,
            <strong>Dr. Bilal Ahmad</strong>, who teaches at
            <strong>UET Lahore, Faisalabad Campus</strong>.
            In one of our sessions, he encouraged us to establish an online presence
            as early as possible in our academic careers.
            He told us that in today's world,
            your digital footprint matters just as much as your grades.
            Employers, collaborators, and researchers look you up online before they ever
            meet you in person.
            If there is nothing to find, you are essentially invisible.
            That simple piece of advice set me on this path,
            and I have not looked back since.
        </p>

            By hosting my blog there,
            I am already speaking the language of the industry before I even graduate.

        <h2>💡 Programming Fundamentals — Building the Mindset Before the Code</h2>

        <p>
            Programming Fundamentals was the course where many of us truly began to think
            like engineers.
            It is one thing to learn syntax,
            but it is another thing entirely to learn how to break a problem down,
            approach it logically,
            and write code that actually solves it cleanly.
            Dr. Bilal Ahmad made sure we understood the difference.
        </p>

        <p>
            We covered the core building blocks—variables,
            loops,
            conditionals,
            functions,
            and the logic that binds them together.
            But more importantly,
            we were pushed to practice,
            make mistakes,
            and understand why those mistakes happened.
            Programming is a craft,
            and like every craft,
            it demands repetition,
            patience,
            and reflection.
        </p>

        <p>
            This portfolio itself is a product of that course.
            The fact that I can build a GitHub Pages website,
            organize articles,
            and think about readability and discoverability
            comes directly from the foundational mindset that Programming Fundamentals
            introduced.
        </p>

        <hr>
                <h2>🗄️ Database Systems — Understanding How the World Stores Its Knowledge</h2>

        <p>
            The second course Dr. Bilal Ahmad taught us this semester was
            <strong>Database Systems</strong>.
            If Programming Fundamentals taught us how to think,
            Database Systems taught us how to organize.
            The modern world runs on data.
            Every application,
            every website,
            every mobile app,
            and every online service relies on databases to store,
            retrieve,
            and manage information efficiently.
        </p>

        <p>
            Throughout the semester,
            we learned the principles of relational databases,
            SQL,
            normalization,
            entity-relationship (ER) modeling,
            primary keys,
            foreign keys,
            and structured database design.
            These concepts allowed us to understand how large systems maintain data
            consistency,
            eliminate redundancy,
            and provide reliable access to information.
        </p>

        <p>
            Dr. Bilal Ahmad constantly connected these topics to real-world
            Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning applications.
            He explained that before any intelligent model can produce accurate predictions,
            the underlying data must be organized,
            cleaned,
            validated,
            and stored correctly.
            A poorly designed database leads to poor-quality datasets,
            and poor-quality datasets inevitably produce unreliable machine learning models.
        </p>

        <p>
            This perspective completely changed how I viewed databases.
            They are not simply collections of tables;
            they are the foundation upon which intelligent software systems are built.
            Understanding databases has strengthened my appreciation for software architecture
            and has shown me that successful engineering begins with well-organized information.
        </p>
